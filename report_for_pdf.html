Executive Abstract

This report documents the rigorous numerical modeling and multi-platform simulation of quantum resonant tunnelling in semiconductor double-barrier heterostructures (AlxGa1-xAs / GaAs / AlxGa1-xAs) and its application in Resonant Tunnelling Diodes (RTDs). Using an exact piecewise Transfer Matrix Method (TMM) with BenDaniel-Duke boundary conditions, we solve the 1D Schrödinger equation to obtain transmission spectra T(E), quasi-bound state energies En, and dwell times τ. Macroscopic current transport is modeled via the Tsu-Esaki formula at 300 K, reproducing the characteristic N-shaped Negative Differential Resistance (NDR) curve with a peak-to-valley current ratio (PVCR) of 6.78 and peak current density Jp = 1.18 × 105 A/cm2. Dynamic 2D/3D wavepacket animations based on the Split-Step Fourier Method (SSFM) illustrate coherent cavity accumulation on resonance versus total reflection off-resonance. The project has been deployed across WebGL, Python, and MATLAB frameworks.

1. Executive Summary & Problem Motivation

As conventional silicon semiconductor scaling approaches fundamental quantum-mechanical boundaries—dictated by gate oxide tunneling leakage, subthreshold swing saturation (60 mV/dec at 300 K), and thermal dissipation densities—solid-state device research has increasingly focused on quantum-effect devices that exploit wave mechanics rather than suppressing them.

1.1 The Ultra-High Frequency Terahertz Frontier

Conventional semiconductor transit-time devices (e.g., Gunn diodes, IMPATT diodes, and high-electron-mobility transistors) are intrinsically limited by carrier drift velocity saturation across macroscopic depletion regions (~107 cm/s in GaAs), imposing practical cutoff frequencies below 500 GHz. In contrast, carrier transport in Resonant Tunnelling Diodes (RTDs) is dictated by coherent quantum mechanical tunneling through nanometer-scale potential barriers. Because carrier dwell times are on femtosecond timescales (τ ~ 50–200 fs), RTDs represent the highest-frequency solid-state electronic sources known, achieving fundamental oscillation frequencies exceeding 1.9–2.0 THz at room temperature.

1.2 Negative Differential Resistance (NDR) & Circuit Multifunctionality

The primary operational hallmark of the RTD is its non-monotonic current-voltage (I-V) curve, which exhibits a distinct region of Negative Differential Resistance (NDR) where differential conductance is negative:

G = dI / dV < 0

This property allows a single two-terminal RTD to perform functions that typically require multiple transistors, including terahertz local oscillators, sub-2 ps pulse generators, and multi-valued logic (MVL) memory cells.

2.1 The Optical Fabry-Pérot Analogy

Resonant tunneling in double barriers is mathematically isomorphic to an optical Fabry-Pérot interferometer. When de Broglie electron waves undergo multiple internal reflections inside the quantum well, constructive interference occurs when round-trip phase accumulation satisfies:

2 k_w L_w + φ_1 + φ_2 = 2 π n, \quad n = 1, 2, 3, \dots

At these eigen-energies, transmission approaches unity ($T \to 1$) and probability density $|\psi(x)|^2$ inside the well is amplified by $> 50\times$ compared to the incident wave.

4. Theoretical Methodology & Numerical Formulations

4.1 Stationary 1D Schrödinger Equation

Carrier motion along the growth axis $x$ is described by the effective-mass Schrödinger equation:

-\frac{\hbar^2}{2} \frac{d}{dx}\left( \frac{1}{m^*(x)} \frac{d\psi(x)}{dx} \right) + V(x) \psi(x) = E \psi(x)

Across heterointerfaces with discontinuous effective mass, the BenDaniel-Duke boundary conditions enforce:

\psi(x^-) = \psi(x^+), \quad \left.\frac{1}{m^*(x^-)} \frac{d\psi}{dx}\right|_{x^-} = \left.\frac{1}{m^*(x^+)} \frac{d\psi}{dx}\right|_{x^+}

4.2 Piecewise Transfer Matrix Method (TMM)

The active region $x \in [0, L_{tot}]$ ($L_{tot} = 2 L_b + L_w$) is sliced into $N$ intervals $\Delta x = L_{tot}/N$. In slice $j$:

V_j = V_{flat}(x_j) - e V_a \left(\frac{x_j}{L_{tot}}\right), \quad k_j = \frac{\sqrt{2 m_j^* (E - V_j)}}{\hbar}

The state vector $\mathbf{w}(x) = [\psi(x), \frac{1}{m^*(x)}\psi'(x)]^T$ propagates via the layer matrix $\mathbf{M}_j$:

\mathbf{M}_j = \begin{pmatrix} \cos(k_j \Delta x) & \frac{m_j^*}{k_j} \sin(k_j \Delta x) \\ -\frac{k_j}{m_j^*} \sin(k_j \Delta x) & \cos(k_j \Delta x) \end{pmatrix}

To avoid $0/0$ indeterminate division when $k_j \to 0$, we use the unnormalized Taylor expansion: $\text{sinc}(k_j \Delta x) = \Delta x - k_j^2 \Delta x^3 / 6$. Matching to asymptotic plane wave solutions in emitter ($\alpha_L = i k_L / m_L^*$) and collector ($\alpha_R = i k_R / m_R^*$) leads to:

t = \frac{2 \alpha_L}{\alpha_L u_1 + u_2}, \quad r = t u_1 - 1, \quad \text{where } \begin{pmatrix} u_1 \\ u_2 \end{pmatrix} = \mathbf{M}_{tot}^{-1} \begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ \alpha_R \end{pmatrix}
T(E) = \frac{\text{Re}(k_R) / m_R^*}{k_L / m_L^*} |t|^2, \quad R(E) = |r|^2, \quad T(E) + R(E) = 1.0000000000

4.3 Tsu-Esaki Diode Current Density Formulation

Macroscopic current transport in the RTD integrates longitudinal transmission $T(E, V_a)$ with the transverse electron supply function:

J(V_a) = \frac{e m^* k_B T_{temp}}{2 \pi^2 \hbar^3} \int_0^\infty T(E, V_a) \ln\left( \frac{1 + \exp\left(\frac{E_F - E}{k_B T_{temp}}\right)}{1 + \exp\left(\frac{E_F - E - e V_a}{k_B T_{temp}}\right)} \right) dE

where $T_{temp} = 300\text{ K}$, $k_B T_{temp} \approx 25.85\text{ meV}$, and $E_F = 40\text{ meV}$. Numerically stable $\text{log1p}$ functions prevent numerical overflow.

4.4 Split-Step Fourier Method (SSFM) for Dynamic TDSE

To simulate real-time wavepacket motion in 2D space $(x, y)$, the Time-Dependent Schrödinger Equation is advanced via symmetric Strang splitting:

\Psi(x, y, t + \Delta t) \approx e^{-\frac{i V(x, y) \Delta t}{2 \hbar}} \mathcal{F}^{-1}\left[ e^{-\frac{i \hbar (k_x^2 + k_y^2) \Delta t}{2 m^*}} \mathcal{F}\left[ e^{-\frac{i V(x, y) \Delta t}{2 \hbar}} \Psi(x, y, t) \right] \right]

5. Results, Parametric Analysis & Physical Discussion

5.1 Baseline Resonant Cavity Performance

For a baseline heterostructure ($V_0 = 0.30\text{ eV}$, $L_b = 3.0\text{ nm}$, $L_w = 5.0\text{ nm}$, $m_w^* = 0.067 m_0$, $m_b^* = 0.092 m_0$ at $V_a = 0\text{ V}$):

Physical Parameter Computed Value Physical Meaning
Ground Resonance $E_1$ $0.0880\text{ eV}$ ($88.0\text{ meV}$) Lowest quasi-bound eigenstate inside quantum well.
Resonance Linewidth $\Gamma$ $0.0040\text{ eV}$ ($4.0\text{ meV}$) Energy broadening (FWHM) due to barrier coupling.
Quasi-Bound Lifetime $\tau$ $164.7\text{ fs}$ ($1.65 \times 10^{-13}\text{ s}$) Mean dwell time of trapped electron wavepacket ($\tau = \hbar / \Gamma$).
Peak Transmission $T_{max}$ $0.9998 \approx 1.00$ Complete constructive de Broglie interference in cavity.
Cavity Quality Factor $Q$ $22.0$ Sharpness of resonance: $Q = E_1 / \Gamma$.
Well Amplification $54.2 \times |\psi_{inc}|^2$ Standing wave probability density buildup in well.

5.2 Parametric Sensitivity Analysis

  • Barrier Height ($V_0$): Increasing $V_0$ from $0.20$ to $0.60\text{ eV}$ increases barrier attenuation ($\kappa = \sqrt{2 m_b^* (V_0 - E)}/\hbar$), suppressing non-resonant leakage below the barrier crest ($T < 10^{-6}$).
  • Barrier Thickness ($L_b$): Linewidth narrows exponentially: $\Gamma \propto \exp(-2 \kappa L_b)$. Increasing $L_b$ from $2.0$ to $4.0\text{ nm}$ reduces $\Gamma$ from $18.5\text{ meV}$ ($\tau \approx 35\text{ fs}$) to $0.85\text{ meV}$ ($\tau \approx 775\text{ fs}$), boosting PVCR at the cost of current amplitude.
  • Well Width ($L_w$): Resonant energies scale as $E_n \propto 1/L_w^2$. Widening $L_w$ lowers $E_1$ and brings $E_2$ into the well for $L_w > 6.5\text{ nm}$, enabling multi-state logic.

5.3 Resonant Tunnelling Diode $I-V$ & NDR Operation

Applying the Tsu-Esaki equation over bias $V_a \in [0, 0.35\text{ V}]$ reveals the four operational regimes of the RTD:

  1. Thermal Equilibrium ($V_a = 0\text{ V}$): $J = 0$. $E_1 = 88\text{ meV}$ sits above the emitter Fermi sea ($E_F = 40\text{ meV}$).
  2. Resonant Peak ($V_a = 0.14\text{ V}$): Conduction band tilts by $e V_a / 2$, pulling $E_1$ into alignment with occupied emitter states ($0 < E_1 < E_F$). Current surges to:
    J_{peak} = 1.18 \times 10^5\text{ A/cm}^2 \quad \text{at } V_{peak} = 0.140\text{ V}
  3. Negative Differential Resistance (NDR) ($0.14\text{ V} < V_a < 0.20\text{ V}$): $E_1$ drops below the emitter band edge ($E_c$). Resonant tunneling quenches because emitter electrons cannot conserve transverse momentum, producing $dJ/dV_a < 0$.
  4. Valley Point ($V_a = 0.20\text{ V}$): Current drops to a minimum $J_{valley} = 1.74 \times 10^4\text{ A/cm}^2$, yielding a Peak-to-Valley Current Ratio:
    \text{PVCR} = \frac{J_{peak}}{J_{valley}} = \frac{1.18 \times 10^5}{1.74 \times 10^4} \approx 6.78

7. Main Conclusions

Summary of Core Scientific Findings
  1. Validation of Coherent Resonant Tunnelling: The Transfer Matrix Method proved that constructive interference in the well amplifies probability density by $> 54\times$, yielding near-unity transmission ($T \to 1.00$) at $E_1 = 88.0\text{ meV}$.
  2. Origin of Macroscopic NDR: The N-shaped I-V curve was directly mapped to sub-band alignment and subsequent cutoff against the emitter Fermi sea, demonstrating $J_p = 1.18 \times 10^5\text{ A/cm}^2$ and $\text{PVCR} \approx 6.78$.
  3. Linewidth-Lifetime Trade-off: Dwell times follow $\tau = \hbar/\Gamma$, scaling exponentially with barrier thickness ($L_b$), governing the fundamental trade-off between switching speed and current density.
  4. Open-Source Dissemination: A tri-platform suite was published under the MIT License on GitHub with a 1-click live web simulation.

8. Future Scope & Research Extensions

  • Self-Consistent Poisson-Schrödinger Solver: Incorporate dynamic space-charge accumulation inside the quantum well at high current densities to capture electrostatic hysteresis and intrinsic bistability.
  • Non-Equilibrium Green's Function (NEGF) Formalism: Extend beyond coherent tunneling to model dissipative processes (optical phonons, acoustic phonons, interface roughness).
  • High-Offset Heterostructures: Investigate In0.53Ga0.47As / AlAs and InAs / AlSb systems ($V_0 > 1.2\text{ eV}$) to achieve ultra-high $\text{PVCR} > 25$ for room-temperature THz logic.
  • Quantum Cascade Lasers (QCLs): Expand to periodic superlattices (20–50 periods) for intersubband mid-infrared and THz optical sources.
  • WebGPU & Machine Learning Inverse Design: Accelerate 3D TDSE simulations to 60 FPS in the browser using WebGPU, and apply neural networks to automate barrier optimization.
