Resonant Tunnelling in a Double-Barrier Structure:
Transfer Matrix Modeling, Negative Differential Resistance,
and 3D Wavepacket Simulation
Mohammad Azmal Shah, Pavan Karthik, Anant Goel, Darshan Mittal
School of Engineering & Technology, BML Munjal University
Abstract

Resonant tunnelling through semiconductor double-barrier heterostructures represents a fundamental mechanism for ultra-high-speed nanoelectronics, providing coherent transit times on femtosecond timescales and macroscopic Negative Differential Resistance (NDR) suitable for terahertz (THz) oscillators and multi-valued logic. However, analytical modeling of tilted potential profiles and intuitive pedagogical visualization of wavepacket dynamics remain significant challenges. In this paper, we formulate an exact numerical Transfer Matrix Method (TMM) incorporating BenDaniel-Duke boundary conditions for position-dependent effective mass to solve the stationary 1D Schrödinger equation under arbitrary applied bias. Using this framework, we systematically investigate the sensitivity of transmission spectra T(E), eigen-energies En, linewidths Γ, and quasi-bound state dwell times τ to changes in barrier height (V0), barrier thickness (Lb), and well width (Lw). The macroscopic current transport of a room-temperature (300 K) Resonant Tunnelling Diode (RTD) is computed via the Tsu-Esaki formula, demonstrating a peak current density of 1.18 × 105 A/cm2 at 0.14 V and a peak-to-valley current ratio (PVCR) of 6.78. Furthermore, we develop a dynamic solver based on the Split-Step Fourier Method (SSFM) to simulate Time-Dependent Schrödinger Equation (TDSE) wavepacket propagation in 2D and 3D, directly visualizing cavity buildup on resonance versus total reflection off-resonance. The complete simulation environment is implemented across three interoperable frameworks: a zero-dependency Python desktop GUI, an automated MATLAB script suite, and an interactive 3D WebGL web application deployed on GitHub Pages.

I. Introduction

As modern complementary metal-oxide-semiconductor (CMOS) field-effect transistors approach fundamental quantum-mechanical boundaries—governed by direct gate-oxide tunneling leakage, subthreshold swing saturation (60 mV/dec at 300 K), and severe localized power dissipation—solid-state research has increasingly targeted quantum-effect devices that exploit wave mechanics rather than attempting to suppress them [1], [2].

Conventional semiconductor transit-time devices such as Gunn diodes, IMPATT diodes, and high-electron-mobility transistors (HEMTs) are fundamentally constrained by the drift velocity saturation of carriers across macroscopic depletion regions (~107 cm/s in GaAs), imposing practical operating frequency cutoffs below 500 GHz [3]. In contrast, carrier transport in Resonant Tunnelling Diodes (RTDs) is governed by coherent quantum-mechanical tunneling across nanometer-scale potential barriers. Because this transit process is determined by quasi-bound state dwell times on femtosecond timescales (τ ~ 50–200 fs), RTDs represent the highest-frequency solid-state electronic sources currently known, achieving fundamental continuous-wave oscillation frequencies exceeding 1.9–2.0 THz at room temperature [4].

The central electrical characteristic of the RTD is its non-monotonic current-voltage (I-V) curve, which exhibits a distinct region of Negative Differential Resistance (NDR) where differential conductance is negative:

G=dIdV<0G = \frac{dI}{dV} < 0G=dVdI​<0
(1)

This unique physical property allows a single two-terminal RTD to perform circuit functions that conventionally require multiple transistors, including terahertz local oscillators, sub-2 ps pulse generators, and multi-valued logic (MVL) memory cells [5].

Despite the foundational importance of resonant tunneling, visualizing the complex interplay between de Broglie wave interference, complex eigen-energies, and voltage-dependent band bending remains difficult with static 1D textbook diagrams. In this project, we address this challenge by delivering an exact Transfer Matrix solver, a Tsu-Esaki diode transport engine, and real-time 3D wavepacket animations deployed across multiple platforms.

When de Broglie electron waves undergo multiple internal reflections inside the quantum well, constructive interference occurs when round-trip phase accumulation satisfies:

where kw is the electron wavevector in the well, Lw is the well width, and φ1, φ2 are reflection phase shifts at the barriers. At these eigen-energies, transmission approaches unity (T → 1) despite thick barriers, and the probability density |ψ(x)|2 inside the well is amplified by orders of magnitude compared to the incident beam.

IV. Results and Analysis

A. Baseline Resonant Cavity Performance

A standard GaAs quantum well clad by Al0.3Ga0.7As barriers was simulated with baseline parameters: V0 = 0.30 eV, Lb = 3.0 nm, Lw = 5.0 nm, mw* = 0.067 m0, and mb* = 0.092 m0 at Va = 0 V. Table II summarizes the extracted metrics.

B. Parametric Sensitivity Analysis

Systematic parameter sweeps revealed clear physical scaling laws:

  • Barrier Height (V0): Increasing V0 from 0.20 to 0.60 eV increases the barrier attenuation coefficient κ = [2 mb* (V0 - E)]1/2 / ℏ, exponentially suppressing non-resonant sub-barrier leakage transmission below 10-6.
  • Barrier Thickness (Lb): Linewidth Γ decreases exponentially: Γ(Lb) ∝ exp(-2 κ Lb). Increasing Lb from 2.0 to 4.0 nm narrows Γ from 18.5 meV (τ ≈ 35 fs) to 0.85 meV (τ ≈ 775 fs), sharply increasing cavity Q at the cost of current amplitude.
  • Well Width (Lw): Eigen-energies scale as En ∝ 1 / Lw2. Expanding Lw lowers E1 and introduces higher-order resonant states (E2, E3) for Lw > 6.5 nm.

D. 3D Time-Dependent Wavepacket Dynamics

The 2D/3D Split-Step Fourier solver confirmed distinct quantum transit regimes:

  • On-Resonance (E0 = E1 = 0.088 eV): The incident wavepacket enters the quantum well, establishes a standing wave oscillation cavity over t ≈ 50–120 fs, and emits a coherent transmitted wavepacket into the collector.
  • Off-Resonance (E0 = 0.044 eV): Over 99% of the incident probability density reflects off the first barrier, leaving zero standing wave in the well and zero transmitted flux.

VI. Conclusion and Future Work

A. Main Conclusions

This research has systematically modeled, simulated, and visualized resonant tunneling in double-barrier heterostructures and Resonant Tunnelling Diodes:

  1. Verification of Coherent Resonant Tunnelling: The Transfer Matrix Method rigorously proved that constructive interference in the quantum well amplifies internal probability density by > 54×, yielding near-unity transmission (T → 1.00) at E1 = 88.0 meV.
  2. Origin of Macroscopic NDR: The N-shaped I-V characteristic was directly mapped to sub-band alignment and subsequent cutoff against the emitter Fermi sea, demonstrating Jp = 1.18 × 105 A/cm2 and PVCR ≈ 6.78.
  3. Linewidth-Lifetime Trade-off: Dwell times follow τ = ℏ / Γ, scaling exponentially with barrier thickness (Lb), establishing the fundamental trade-off between switching speed and current density.
  4. Open-Source Dissemination: A tri-platform suite was published under the MIT License on GitHub with a live 1-click web simulation.

B. Future Work

  • Self-Consistent Poisson-Schrödinger Solver: Incorporate dynamic space-charge accumulation in the quantum well to model electrostatic hysteresis and intrinsic bistability.
  • Non-Equilibrium Green's Function (NEGF) Formalism: Account for dissipative optical/acoustic phonon scattering and interface roughness.
  • High-Offset Heterostructures: Investigate InGaAs/AlAs and InAs/AlSb systems (V0 > 1.2 eV) to achieve PVCR > 25 for room-temperature THz logic.
  • Quantum Cascade Lasers (QCLs): Expand to periodic superlattices (20–50 periods) for mid-infrared and THz optical sources.
